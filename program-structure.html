Program Structure

Your Doctoral Journey

Each year of the program builds deliberately on the last — from methodological foundations, through specialization and candidacy, to original research and placement.

The program is designed as a full-time research-intensive doctoral experience. Students entering with a master’s degree must complete a minimum of 48 credits; Individual programs of study are developed with faculty advisers and integrate research-methods training, doctoral seminars, specialization courses, qualifying examinations, and dissertation research.

  1. 01

    Year 1

    Research Foundations

    Build foundations in core research methodology and the discipline, participate in doctoral seminars and the research community, establish an academic adviser and program of study, develop research interests, and engage early in faculty research.

  2. 02

    Year 2

    Specialization and Candidacy Preparation

    Complete advanced specialization courses, conduct independent research, prepare for teaching, complete the Qualifying Exam Part I / comprehensive examination, establish a thesis adviser and research direction, and fulfill teaching certificate.

  3. 03

    Year 3

    Dissertation Development

    Develop a dissertation proposal, conduct original research, participate in conferences, develop manuscripts, complete the Qualifying Exam Part II / thesis proposal defense, gain admission to candidacy, and conduct dissertation research.

  4. 04

    Year 4

    Dissertation Completion and Placement

    Complete and defend the dissertation, produce publications, disseminate research, prepare for an academic career, and undertake academic job-market preparation.

Key milestones

Students progress through key milestones, including:

  • A program of study tailored to the student’s track and research interests.

  • Advanced coursework in research methods and the chosen specialization.

  • Qualifying Exam Part I: A comprehensive examination assessing knowledge in the student’s field and related areas.

  • Qualifying Exam Part II: An oral defense of the doctoral thesis proposal.

  • Original dissertation research conducted under faculty supervision and reviewed by a doctoral thesis committee.

  • Successful defense of a dissertation that makes an original scholarly contribution.

  • Completion of AUB-wide doctoral requirements, including teaching preparation and applicable residence, academic standing, and graduation requirements.

Research Mentoring & Scholarly Community

Your doctoral research develops through close mentorship, collaboration, and scholarly exchange.

Mentorship is central to the doctoral experience. During the early stages of the program, you will receive academic advising to develop your program of study and identify potential research directions. As your research agenda develops, you will work closely with a thesis adviser and doctoral thesis committee.

You will also be an active member of OSB’s scholarly community through faculty research collaboration, research seminars, workshops, visiting-scholar activities, conferences, and opportunities to present work in progress and receive feedback from faculty and peers.

Teaching & Academic Career Development

Develop not only as a researcher, but also as an educator and future member of the academic community.

You will complete AUB’s required preparation in teaching in higher education and gain supervised teaching experience and instructional opportunities.

Professional development will also include scholarly writing, conference presentations, publication training, research ethics, networking, and preparation for the international academic job market.

International Research Exposure

Build scholarly connections beyond borders.

You will be encouraged to develop international research networks through conferences, collaborative research, and engagement with visiting and partner scholars.

Subject to academic approval and partnership arrangements, opportunities may include spending a semester at an international partner institution, taking approved courses, and/or advancing your research under complementary academic mentorship.
