The Program

Program Structure and Doctoral Journey

The program is designed as a full-time research-intensive doctoral experience. Students entering with a master’s degree complete a minimum of 48 credits beyond the master’s degree; exceptionally qualified students admitted through the accelerated track from a bachelor’s degree complete a minimum of 78 credits, in accordance with AUB Graduate Council requirements. Individual programs of study are developed with faculty advisers and integrate research-methods training, doctoral seminars, specialization courses, qualifying examinations, and dissertation research.

Stages

Stage, primary focus, and key milestones

Stage Primary focus Key milestones
Year 1 – Research Foundations Core research methodology, disciplinary foundations, doctoral seminars, research community participation Academic adviser and program of study; development of research interests; early faculty research engagement
Year 2 – Specialization and Candidacy Preparation Advanced specialization courses, independent research, teaching preparation Qualifying Exam Part I / comprehensive examination; thesis adviser and research direction; teaching certificate requirements
Year 3 – Dissertation Development Dissertation proposal, original research, conference participation, manuscript development Qualifying Exam Part II / thesis proposal defense; admission to candidacy; dissertation research
Year 4 – Dissertation Completion and Placement Completion and defense of dissertation, publications, academic career preparation Dissertation defense; dissemination of research; academic job-market preparation
Degree & Candidacy

Degree and Candidacy Milestones

Seven required checkpoints guide each doctoral student from entry to graduation.

01

A program of study tailored to the student’s track and research interests.

02

Advanced coursework in research methods and the chosen specialization.

03

Qualifying Exam Part I: a comprehensive examination assessing knowledge in the student’s field and related areas.

04

Qualifying Exam Part II: an oral defense of the doctoral thesis proposal.

05

Original dissertation research conducted under faculty supervision and reviewed by a doctoral thesis committee.

06

Successful defense of a dissertation that makes an original scholarly contribution.

07

Completion of AUB-wide doctoral requirements, including teaching preparation and applicable residence, academic standing, and graduation requirements.

Beyond the Classroom

Mentoring, teaching, and international exposure

Research Mentoring and Scholarly Community

Mentorship is central to the doctoral experience. During the early stage of the program, students receive academic advising to develop an appropriate program of study and identify research directions. As their research agenda develops, students work closely with a thesis adviser and a doctoral thesis committee in accordance with AUB regulations.

Doctoral students are expected to be active members of OSB’s scholarly community. Their development is supported through faculty research collaboration, research seminars, workshops, visiting-scholar activities, conference participation, and opportunities to present work in progress and receive feedback from faculty and peers.

Suggested website modules: Research Seminar Series | Research Centers and Initiatives | Doctoral Student Research | Recent Faculty Publications | Research Resources at AUB

Teaching and Academic Career Development

The program prepares students not only to conduct high-quality research but also to become effective university educators. Doctoral students complete AUB’s required preparation in teaching in higher education and gain supervised teaching experience through graduate assistantship and instructional opportunities. Professional development also includes scholarly writing, conference presentations, publication training, research ethics, networking, and preparation for the international academic job market.

International Research Exposure

Students are encouraged to build international scholarly networks through conference participation, collaborative research, and engagement with visiting and partner scholars in their field. Subject to academic approval and partnership arrangements, students may have the opportunity to spend a semester at a partner institution, take approved courses, and/or advance their research under complementary academic mentorship.
